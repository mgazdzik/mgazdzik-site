CxMS

Closed beta

A lightweight Context Management System for teams building with AI.

Most AI projects start from a pile of Jira tickets and Slack scrollback standing in for real context - the decisions, constraints, and reasoning nobody wrote down. The agent doesn't have it, and three weeks in, neither does the team.

CxMS is my attempt at making product context something you actually capture, maintain, and share on purpose - not something you reconstruct after the fact, badly, right when an agent needs it most.

It's in closed beta right now. I'm building it in the open - the calls I'm making, what's working, what isn't - mostly on LinkedIn.

ez-pollster

A survey tool that lives inside your AI agent instead of a browser tab.

Both creating and answering surveys happen entirely through MCP - no forms, no browser flow for respondents, no separate app to check. If you and the people you're surveying already work through an agent, the survey should too.

I open-sourced the product thinking behind it, not just the code: a full blueprint with positioning, decisions, features, and build sequence - ezpollster-blueprint. Feed it to your own coding agent and build your version from it, not a copy of mine.

That's the actual pitch: not “here's my repo,” but “here's how I thought about it - go think about yours.”

Product Owner

Co warto wiedzieć o tej roli, a mało się o tym mówi.

I wrote this after nearly four years as Product Owner at Brand24, during the company's move to agile. I went looking for what to expect going in and mostly found tool lists, certifications, and courses - not much about the actual texture of the job. So I wrote the version I wished I'd had, mistakes included. If “Owner” is in the title, the failures should be owned too.

01

Role or position? Why treating PO as a role - not a title - changes how you show up, and the “missionary vs. mercenary” split in how people lead.

02

Thinking in systems. Everything in a product is connected; decisions in one corner ripple everywhere else.

03

Getting your footing in a new org. Reading the org chart, the stakeholder map, and your own gaps before you start making calls.

04

Responsibility. Why authority has to be earned through owning outcomes - including the bad ones - not claimed on day one.

05

No sentiment in business. Budget is time, time is the team's hours, and every product decision is quietly a budget decision.

06

The iron triangle. Scope, cost, and time trade off against each other - “the best solution” almost never exists, only the best one for this situation.

07

What a PO actually needs to know. A working map across analytics, strategy, UX, marketing, customer success, leadership, sales, and technology - not mastery, just enough to ask the right questions.

08

Taming the chaos. Automation, delegation, time-blocking, and a task-list system that survives a day full of interruptions.

09

Plans vs. planning. Why a roadmap is a living, regularly-updated best guess, not a promise carved in stone - learned the hard way after promising three features on fixed dates and delivering one.

10

Transparency, inspection, adaptation. Scrum's three pillars, applied past Scrum - to 1:1s, to habits, to basically anything you're trying to steer.
